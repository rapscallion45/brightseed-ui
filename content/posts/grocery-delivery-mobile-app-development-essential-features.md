A grocery delivery app connects ordering with a physical operation. A clear checkout is important, but customers also need accurate availability, understandable substitutions and a delivery promise the business can fulfil.

## Map the order from start to finish

Document how a customer finds products, chooses quantities, checks delivery availability, pays and receives updates. Then map what staff must do to pick, pack and dispatch that same order.

Identify the source of stock information and how frequently it changes. Decide what happens when an item becomes unavailable between checkout and picking.

## Prioritise the customer journey

A useful first release may include:

- Search and categories that help people find products.
- Clear prices, quantities, availability and delivery charges.
- A basket that can be reviewed before payment.
- Delivery slots based on the operation's capacity.
- Substitution preferences and a way to approve changes.
- Order status, cancellation rules and a route to support.

Payment does not mean immediate delivery. Communicate the available service and any cut-off times accurately throughout the journey.

## Give each role the access it needs

Customers, store staff, dispatchers, couriers and administrators have different tasks. They may need different interfaces, but they do not automatically need four separate applications.

Define what each role can view and change. Staff might manage stock and picking; couriers may need delivery details and status updates. Avoid exposing information that is unnecessary for the task.

## Plan for exceptions

Consider a failed payment, a missing item, an inaccessible address or a customer who is unavailable. Agree how refunds, substitutions and delivery problems are handled before turning the workflow into software.

Keep an audit trail of important order changes and make it possible for support staff to understand what happened. Integrations need a recovery path when data or services are temporarily unavailable.

## Test with the people doing the work

Review the prototype with shoppers and operational staff. Test a realistic order with substitutions and a delivery change, not only a small successful purchase.
