You can lead an application project without writing its code. Your first job is to explain the problem, understand the users and make decisions about the product. Technical help can then be matched to a clear need.

## Describe a useful outcome

Write down who the app is for, what they need to do and why the current approach is difficult. Avoid starting with a list of technologies. A short example of a real task is often more helpful than an extensive feature list.

For example: a customer needs to choose an available appointment, confirm it and receive instructions. That gives a designer and developer something concrete to explore.

## Sketch the main journey

Use paper or simple slides to show the screens and decisions. Include what happens when information is missing, a payment fails or a user needs help. Review the sketch with someone who would actually use the product.

An interactive prototype can make the journey easier to test. It does not need a complete backend and should not be mistaken for a finished product.

## Compare ways to build

- A configurable product may already cover the task.
- A no-code tool may suit a bounded workflow, subject to its integrations, permissions and export options.
- A custom application can address more specific requirements, with greater responsibility for development and maintenance.

Learning to code is another path if that is something you want to do, but it is not a prerequisite for commissioning an application. Ask a technical partner to explain the trade-offs in ordinary language.

## Prepare a brief and budget

Include the main journey, required integrations, timing and the limits of the first release. Ask what is included in testing, deployment and handover. Plan for operating costs and support alongside the build.

Funding can come from several sources; attracting investors is not a mandatory development step. Decide what evidence you need before committing more money.

## Stay involved

Review working features, record decisions and agree how changes affect scope. Keep control of business accounts and know where the code and documentation will live.
