A social network needs a reason for people to join and return. Start with a specific community and a useful interaction rather than a checklist of features copied from a large platform.

## Define the community and its purpose

Who is the service for? What can members do there that is difficult elsewhere? An internal professional community, a local interest group and a public publishing network have different access, privacy and moderation needs.

Talk to potential members before committing to a build. Test whether the intended interaction matters to them and how they currently meet that need.

## Choose a focused first release

A possible first release might include accounts, member profiles, a way to publish content and a way to respond. Private messaging, payments, live video and recommendation systems should each earn their place in the scope.

A prototype can help test the experience without a complete backend. A minimum viable product, or MVP, is a working version that delivers enough value to learn from real use. They answer different questions.

## Plan moderation and permissions early

Decide who can see, create, edit and remove each type of content. Plan reporting, blocking, account recovery and a process for reviewing abuse reports. Set boundaries for what personal information is visible to other members.

Moderation is also an operating responsibility. Decide who will review reports and how users can appeal or ask for help. Features that create ongoing work need an owner after launch.

## Design the technical foundations

The application needs a data model for members, content and relationships. Consider media storage, search, notifications, rate limits and how external services fail. Keep privileged decisions on the server and test that users cannot access another person's private data.

Start with an architecture the team can operate. Choose technologies based on the requirements and maintenance skills rather than claims that one language is always best.

## Estimate a defined scope

There is no useful universal price for a social network. A small private community and a public platform with media, payments and live messaging are different projects. Break an estimate into design, development, testing, launch and operation; identify the assumptions behind each.

After launch, review whether members complete the intended activity and return. Use their feedback alongside the community's purpose to choose the next improvements.
