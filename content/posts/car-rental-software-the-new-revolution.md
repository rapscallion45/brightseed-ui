Car rental software should connect a reservation with the real availability and condition of a vehicle. Start by mapping the rental operation; ride-hailing features such as driver-passenger chat are not requirements for every rental business.

## Define the rental model

Clarify whether customers reserve a specific vehicle or a category, which locations support collection and return, and how staff handle extensions. These decisions affect availability, pricing and the information shown during booking.

Document when a vehicle is unavailable because of cleaning, maintenance or an existing reservation. A booking calendar needs to reflect operational constraints, not just customer dates.

## Design the booking journey

Make the vehicle category, rental period, collection location, included services and charges clear before confirmation. Define how customers receive booking details and request a change.

Payments and deposits should use an appropriate payment-provider integration. Authentication and encryption are parts of a system's controls; neither makes all transactions completely safe. Define access, reconciliation and error handling as part of the project.

## Support staff at handover and return

Staff may need to record vehicle condition, documents, mileage or fuel levels according to the business process. Design these tasks with the people performing them and establish who can correct a record.

The system should make it possible to trace important changes and understand a customer's booking history without exposing unnecessary information.

## Include exceptions in the scope

- A vehicle becomes unavailable after reservation.
- The customer changes the return time or location.
- A payment fails or needs a refund.
- Staff discover damage or a maintenance requirement.
- An external integration is temporarily unavailable.

Decide what should happen in each case and what requires a staff decision. Avoid turning every exception into an automatic charge or a hidden rule.

## Choose software against the workflow

Compare an existing rental platform with a custom build. Consider integrations, data export, support and the effort needed to maintain the system. A custom product is most useful when there is a clear requirement that existing tools do not meet.
